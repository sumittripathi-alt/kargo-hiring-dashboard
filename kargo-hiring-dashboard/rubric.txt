PRODUCT MANAGER RUBRIC  (weights sum to 100)
=====================================================================

Criterion name: Hands-On Operations Time
What a strong candidate looks like: Has personally done day-to-day operational work in freight forwarding, customs/CHA, port, 3PL, carrier or supply-chain operations (e.g. prepared shipping documents, coordinated carriers, resolved shipment exceptions, handled forwarder accounts) for at least 12 months, with specifics such as volumes, document types, or named parties. WEAK: only built software for, sold to, or advised logistics companies without doing the operational work; or logistics appears only as a keyword/skill list.
Weight: 30%

Criterion name: Built It Unasked, Others Kept Using It
What a strong candidate looks like: Names at least one tool, process, template or framework they started without being assigned, AND states that people beyond their own immediate team adopted or kept using it (who/how many/how long). Two or more such examples = top score. WEAK: improvements that were part of the assigned job, or adoption by only their own small team, or no evidence anyone used it.
Weight: 30%

Criterion name: Owned It With No Safety Net
What a strong candidate looks like: Explicitly owned a product, account, process or function as the only or most senior person (sole owner, "no manager/layer above", no escalations) and made the calls themselves. WEAK: worked as one member of a larger team, presented to seniors for approval, or ownership is implied only by a title.
Weight: 20%

Criterion name: Writes Down What Failed
What a strong candidate looks like: Shows at least one case where they killed a feature/initiative, ran a post-mortem, or documented a root cause of a loss or outage, and something changed afterwards (a process, a decision, a shared write-up). WEAK: only success stories; failures absent or mentioned without any consequence.
Weight: 20%

=====================================================================
SENIOR PRODUCT MANAGER RUBRIC  (weights sum to 100; bar is higher, especially on independence)
=====================================================================

Criterion name: Hands-On Operations Time
What a strong candidate looks like: At least 24 months of personally doing operational work in freight forwarding, customs/CHA, port, 3PL, carrier or supply-chain operations, and can show they understand the integration/data side of it (e.g. carrier or port-system coordination, FMS/TMS migrations, EDI/document data flows). WEAK: under a year of hands-on time, or domain exposure only through building/selling/advising.
Weight: 20%

Criterion name: Built It Unasked, Others Kept Using It
What a strong candidate looks like: At least two self-started tools, processes or frameworks, each with evidence of adoption beyond their own team (other teams, the whole company, or customers), and at least one that became a standing practice that outlasted the initial effort. WEAK: a single example, adoption limited to their own team, or work done because it was assigned.
Weight: 25%

Criterion name: Owned It With No Safety Net
What a strong candidate looks like: Across more than one role or a sustained period, was the sole or most senior owner of a product area, function or account base with nobody above making the calls, including hard trade-off decisions (what to build, stop, or refuse) and accountability for the consequences. Bonus: built or trained a team or set standards others followed. WEAK: senior title but decisions routed through committees, managers or peers; ownership of a slice inside a bigger team.
Weight: 35%

Criterion name: Writes Down What Failed
What a strong candidate looks like: Repeated pattern (two or more instances) of killing work on evidence, running post-mortems, or documenting root causes, where the learning was turned into a process or rule that others now follow. WEAK: one isolated instance, or no changed process afterwards.
Weight: 20%
